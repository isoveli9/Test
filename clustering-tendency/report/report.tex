\section{Methods}

We used Python with NumPy for the computations and Matplotlib for the
figures. The data file contains 1000 three-dimensional vectors with all
values in $[0,1]$ (checked in the code). For the entropies, a subset of $k$
dimensions was divided into $m=64$ equal grid cells by splitting each of
its dimensions into $64^{1/k}$ equal ranges (4, 8 and 64 ranges for $k=3$,
2 and 1; the last range includes the value 1). The greedy searches were
simulated with the seven resulting entropies.

Component~0 (Figure~\ref{fig:hist}) has a peak at about 0.25 and a broad

component~2 contributes most to the clustering tendency, component~0 some
and component~1 nothing.

Components~0 and~1 (Figure~\ref{fig:scatter}) show no groups: the density

Components~0 and~2 show three compact, well-separated clusters with roughly

or 0.75, and when component~2 is high, around 0.5. Neither component alone
shows all three clusters: component~2 merges the two lower clusters, and in
component~0 the upper cluster fills the gap between the lower two. The pair
therefore has a clearly stronger clustering tendency than either component.

Components~1 and~2 show two horizontal bands (component~2 $\approx0.25$ and
$\approx0.75$) spread evenly over component~1, i.e.\ only the bimodality of
component~2 and no dependence on component~1.

Component~1 is thus uniform noise, independent of the other components.
Including it stretches each cluster over the whole component-1 axis and
dilutes the clustering tendency; the cluster structure lies in the subspace
of components~0 and~2.

\section{Entropies}

With $p_i$ the fraction of points in cell $i$, the probability-based
entropy (Aggarwal, Section~6.2.1.3, eq.~(6.2)) is
\[
  E=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl[p_i\ln p_i+(1-p_i)\ln(1-p_i)\bigr],
\]
with $0\ln0=0$. Since $m=64$ for every subset, all values share the same
range: from 0 (all points in one cell) to
$E_{\max}=\ln64-63\ln\frac{63}{64}\approx5.151$ (all $p_i=1/64$).

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\caption{Probability-based entropies of all combinations of dimensions ($m=64$).}
\label{tab:entropies}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccc}
\toprule
Dimensions & $\{0,1,2\}$ & $\{0,1\}$ & $\{0,2\}$ & $\{1,2\}$ & $\{0\}$ & $\{1\}$ & $\{2\}$ \\
\midrule
$E$ & 4.794 & 4.998 & 4.482 & 4.792 & 4.954 & 5.118 & 4.761 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

All values in Table~\ref{tab:entropies} lie in a narrow range, 4.482--5.118
(87--99\,\% of $E_{\max}$), but the differences are consistent.
$E_{\{0,2\}}$ is clearly the lowest, so $\{0,2\}$ has the strongest
clustering tendency. $E_{\{1\}}$ is within 1\,\% of $E_{\max}$
(practically uniform), and adding component~1 always increases the entropy
(by 0.044 for $\{0\}$, 0.031 for $\{2\}$ and 0.312 for $\{0,2\}$): with $m$
fixed, an extra dimension lowers the resolution of the others without
adding structure. Component~2 has the lowest single entropy (4.761), and
adding it always decreases the entropy (by 0.472 for $\{0\}$, 0.326 for
$\{1\}$ and 0.204 for $\{0,1\}$), so it is the strongest single
contributor. Component~0 alone is moderate (4.954) but lowers the entropy
of $\{2\}$ by 0.279, so it contributes mainly together with component~2.

\section{Greedy backward selection}

Starting from all dimensions, each stage drops the worst feature, i.e.\ the
one whose removal gives the lowest entropy, until every removal would
increase the entropy (Table~\ref{tab:backward}).

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\caption{Stages of greedy backward selection (lowest candidate in bold).}
\label{tab:backward}
\begin{tabular}{cllc}
\toprule
Stage & Current set ($E$) & Candidates ($E$) & Decision \\
\midrule
1 & $\{0,1,2\}$ (4.794) & $\{1,2\}$: 4.792,\ \ $\{0,2\}$: \textbf{4.482},\ \ $\{0,1\}$: 4.998 & drop 1 \\
2 & $\{0,2\}$ (4.482) & $\{2\}$: \textbf{4.761},\ \ $\{0\}$: 4.954 & stop \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

In stage~1, dropping component~1 gives by far the largest decrease
($-0.312$), whereas dropping component~0 barely changes the entropy
($-0.002$) and dropping component~2 increases it ($+0.204$). In stage~2,
both removals increase the entropy ($+0.279$ and $+0.472$), so the search
stops at $\{0,2\}$ with $E=4.482$.

\section{Greedy forward selection}

Starting from the empty set, each stage adds the best feature, i.e.\ the
one that gives the lowest entropy, until every addition would increase the
entropy (Table~\ref{tab:forward}). The empty set has no entropy on a
64-cell grid, so the first stage takes the single dimension with the lowest
entropy.

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering

\label{tab:forward}

Stage & Current set ($E$) & Candidates ($E$) & Decision \\
\midrule
1 & $\emptyset$ & $\{0\}$: 4.954,\ \ $\{1\}$: 5.118,\ \ $\{2\}$: \textbf{4.761} & add 2 \\
2 & $\{2\}$ (4.761) & $\{0,2\}$: \textbf{4.482},\ \ $\{1,2\}$: 4.792 & add 0 \\
3 & $\{0,2\}$ (4.482) & $\{0,1,2\}$: \textbf{4.794} & stop \\

In stage~1, component~2 has the lowest entropy. In stage~2, adding
component~0 decreases the entropy by 0.279, whereas adding component~1
increases it by 0.031. In stage~3, adding component~1 would increase the
entropy by 0.312, so the search stops at $\{0,2\}$, the same result as
backward selection.

\section{Analysis}

The three approaches agree. Visually, the clusters lie in the plane of
components~0 and~2 and component~1 is uniform noise. The entropies confirm
this: $\{0,2\}$ has the lowest entropy, $\{1\}$ is close to $E_{\max}$, and
adding component~1 always increases the entropy. Both greedy searches end
at $\{0,2\}$, which is also the minimum of all seven values, i.e.\ the
result of an exhaustive search, as expected from the plots. They agree
because the data are easy for greedy search: component~1 is harmful in
every combination, so backward selection drops it first, and components~0
and~2 lower the entropy both alone and together, so forward selection adds
them.

Two limitations are visible. First, with $m$ fixed, the resolution per
dimension falls with the dimensionality (64, 8 and 4 ranges). In the
$4\times4\times4$ grid, the range boundaries 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 cut through
the cluster centres, so $E_{\{0,1,2\}}$ is not lower than $E_{\{1,2\}}$
($+0.002$) although the full data contain all three clusters. Second,
entropy measures non-uniformity rather than cluster separation:
component~0 has a clearly lower entropy than the uniform component~1
although its histogram shows no separated groups, so the visual inspection
is needed to interpret the entropies.

\section{Why 64 bins?}

$64=4^3=8^2=64^1$, so uniform grids with equally many ranges per dimension
give exactly $m=64$ cells for every one-, two- and three-dimensional
subset. Because the entropy depends on $m$ (its maximum is determined by
$m$), $m$ should be the same for all compared subsets, which in general is
only approximately possible with $\lceil m^{1/k}\rceil$ ranges per
dimension. Equal $m$ matters especially for the greedy searches, whose
stopping decisions compare subsets of different dimensionality. In
addition, $1000/64\approx15.6$ points per cell suffice to estimate the
$p_i$.

Other values with the same property are the sixth powers 1, 64, 729,
4096,~\dots\ With $m=1$ every entropy is 0, $m=729=9^3=27^2$ leaves only
1.4 points per cell (even for uniform data about a quarter of the cells
would be empty), and $m=4096$ exceeds the number of points, so 64 is the
only practical choice for these data.

With $d$-dimensional data, an equal $m$ for all subset sizes $1,\dots,d$
requires $m=q^{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,d)}$, i.e.\ at least $2^{12}=4096$
cells for $d=4$ and $2^{60}$ for $d=5$, which is impossible. Moreover, a
$k$-dimensional grid with at least two ranges per dimension has at least
$2^k$ cells, so the number of points needed grows exponentially and most
cells become empty. In practice, $m$ is therefore chosen from the number of
points (e.g.\ at least 5--10 points per cell) with $\lceil m^{1/k}\rceil$
ranges per dimension, and entropies are compared relative to their
maximum; alternatively, the entropy is computed from the one-dimensional
distribution of pairwise distances, whose number of bins does not depend
on the dimensionality.

\section{Conclusions}

The data have a clear clustering tendency only in the subspace of
components~0 and~2, which contains three well-separated clusters.
Component~1 is uniform, independent noise, component~2 is the most
informative single component, and component~0 matters mainly together with
component~2. The probability-based entropies and both greedy searches
identify the same optimal subset, $\{0,2\}$. Grid entropies are, however,
sensitive to the grid resolution and measure non-uniformity rather than
cluster separation, so they should be interpreted together with the visual
inspection.
